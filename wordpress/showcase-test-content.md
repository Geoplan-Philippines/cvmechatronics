## POST — "The 20-Minute Site Survey: How We Spec a Property Before We Quote"

**Where:** Posts → Add New · Content mode: **Form** · Template: **Article (standard)**

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Title | The 20-Minute Site Survey: How We Spec a Property Before We Quote |
| Category | Guides |
| Tags | Site Survey, Gate Automation, App Control |
| Excerpt | Why every CV Mechatronics quote starts with a tape measure, not a catalog — and what a proper twenty-minute site survey actually covers. |

**Form fields:**

**Intro**
```
Every quote we've ever sent started the same way — not with a catalog, but with a tape measure. Here's what actually happens in the twenty minutes before we put a number on paper, and why we won't skip it.
```

**Section 1 — heading**
```
We measure the gate, not the brochure
```

**Section 1 — text**
```
The first ten minutes belong to the entrance. We check the width and weight of the gate, the travel path, and the slope of the driveway — a gate that rolls level behaves nothing like one that climbs.

We also look at what the gate is made of. A lightweight steel frame and a solid hardwood panel can share the same dimensions and need completely different operators. This is where most "cheap online quotes" go wrong.
```

**Section 2 — heading**
```
Power, weather, and the worst day
```

**Section 2 — text**
```
A system that only works on a good day isn't finished. We note where power enters the property, how exposed the gate is to wind and rain, and where water pools during a typhoon-season downpour.

Battery backup is sized from this visit, not guessed later. So is the placement of safety photocells — low enough for a child on a bicycle, not just the bumper of a car.
```

**Section 3 — heading**
```
The app comes last, not first
```

**Section 3 — text**
```
Only after the hardware is spec'd do we talk about control: who gets access, whether the gate should open on approach, which alerts matter at 2am and which can wait until morning.

It's tempting to start with the app. But software is the easy part to change — the motor bolted to your gate is not.
```

**Pull quote**
```
A quote you can trust starts with a tape measure. Anything faster is a guess with a price tag.
```

**Closing paragraph**
```
Book a site survey and we'll walk your property the same way — twenty minutes, no obligation, and a quote based on what your gate actually needs. Send us a message through the contacts page.
```

## PROJECT — "One App, Three Households: A Multi-Generational Home in Alabang"

**Where:** Projects → Add New · Content mode: **Form** · Template: **Case study**

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Title | One App, Three Households: A Multi-Generational Home in Alabang |
| Tags | Residential, Gate Automation, CCTV, App Control, Lighting |
| Excerpt | A gate, cameras, lighting, and one carefully permissioned app for a three-generation household in Alabang — including the family's most-requested feature: a timer. |

**Form fields:**

**The brief**
```
Three generations under one roof — grandparents who turn in early, parents who travel for work, kids who come and go at all hours. The family didn't want more gadgets; they wanted to stop worrying about who was home and whether the gate was closed.

The property already had a solid gate and a few old cameras. What it didn't have was anything tying them together.
```

**What we installed — one item per line**
```
Bi-parting swing gate operators with battery backup
Geofenced arrival for three family cars
Eight ColorVu cameras covering gate, driveway, and garden
Scene-based lighting for evenings and away mode
Individual app access for six family members
View-only access for the household caretaker
```

**The detail that made it**
```
The make-or-break detail wasn't technical — it was permission design. Lolo gets a simple screen: open the gate, see who's at the gate. The parents get everything: logs, alerts, and the ability to let in a delivery from the airport.

We also set the gate to close itself two minutes after the last car leaves — the family's most-requested "feature", borrowed from nothing more than a timer.
```

**The outcome**
```
The group chat about "is the gate closed?" has gone quiet. The cameras caught exactly one incident in three months: a cat, in full color, at 3am.
```
